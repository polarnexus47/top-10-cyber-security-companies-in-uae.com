Cybersecurity Companies for Banks, Fintech and Crypto Firms in the UAE

Editorial Team, Cyber Vendor Research · published: October 2026 · updated: October 2026

Banks, fintech companies and virtual-asset firms in the UAE need three things from a cybersecurity provider: testing that reaches web, mobile and API channels, evidence that maps to their financial regulator, and, for crypto firms, the ability to trace funds on the blockchain. Provider type matters more than brand size. The guide below lists what to test, which regulators set the expectations, five provider types from our UAE ranking and ten questions to ask before you sign.

Automated scanning covers known issues. Business-logic flaws, such as a transfer that can be replayed or a limit that can be bypassed, need manual testing. When you compare quotes, ask what share of the work is manual.

Typical Findings in Financial Applications

Testing of banking, payment and wallet applications tends to surface a recurring set of issues. Broken object-level authorisation lets one customer read or change another customer's data by altering an identifier. Weak limits allow transfers or coupon use beyond intended thresholds. Session and token handling leaves access alive after logout or device change. Business logic flaws allow replays or sequence bypasses, such as skipping a confirmation step. Mobile clients store tokens or personal data in places other apps can read, and APIs return more fields than the interface shows. A provider that explains how it looks for these issues is describing real manual work.

Payment-Card Requirements

If you process, store or transmit card data, PCI DSS v4.x applies. Requirement 11.4 requires external and internal penetration testing at least once every 12 months and after significant changes, and Requirement 11.3 covers vulnerability scanning. Ask the provider whether its report format satisfies your acquirer or assessor.

No single type fits every organisation. A bank that already runs a SOC may add a manual-first testing specialist for application and API work. A fintech without a SOC may start with a managed service and add testing before each major release. A virtual-asset firm usually combines testing with forensics capability.

What a Good Report Contains for a Regulator

A regulator or an acquirer rarely wants the raw tool output. They want a document that shows scope, method and outcome. Check that the report contains an executive summary, the scope and dates, the testing method and standards used, every finding with a CVSS rating and proof-of-concept evidence, a remediation plan with priorities, and a retest section showing which findings were closed. Keep reports and retests together so you can show a complete cycle during an audit. Ask the provider whether it can issue a short attestation letter for third parties without exposing technical detail.

Mobile Banking and Wallet Applications

Mobile channels carry most retail activity. A test of a banking or wallet app should cover the client (storage of tokens and personal data, certificate validation, resistance to tampering), the transport and the backend API behind it. Ask whether the provider tests both Android and iOS builds and whether the backend is part of the scope. The OWASP MASVS gives a structure for the client side, and the OWASP API Security Top 10 does the same for the server side. A test that covers only the client leaves the most serious classes of issues, such as broken authorisation in the API, untested.

Third-Party and Vendor Risk

Banks and fintech firms depend on payment processors, KYC providers, cloud platforms and software vendors. Each connection is a route for an attacker. Include third-party integrations in the test scope where the contract allows, review the access granted to vendors and ask providers how they test partner APIs. For virtual-asset firms, custodians and liquidity providers add further connections that deserve the same review.

Budgeting Across Services

Finance teams usually fund three lines: continuous monitoring, periodic testing and incident readiness. Monitoring is a recurring cost, testing is project-based and tied to releases or regulator cycles, and readiness covers a retainer and exercises. A young fintech can start with testing before launch and a clear response plan, then add monitoring as volumes grow. A bank with a mature SOC puts more of its budget into red team and application testing. Ask providers to price each line separately so you can compare proposals on the same basis.

Ten Questions to Ask a Provider

  1. Which financial regulators have your clients answered to?
  2. Can you show a sample report with proof-of-concept evidence and CVSS ratings?
  3. What share of the test is manual, and what is automated?
  4. Will senior testers do the work, and can I speak to them before the project starts?
  5. How do you test APIs, including object-level authorisation?
  6. Is a retest included, and how long after delivery?
  7. Where are test data and logs stored, and which sub-processors do you use?
  8. How do you report a critical finding during the test?
  9. For crypto firms: which blockchains and bridges can you trace, and what does the forensic report contain?
  10. What do you need from us before the start: accounts, documentation, test environment?

Incident Response for Banking and Virtual-Asset Incidents

A banking incident usually involves account takeover, payment fraud or ransomware, and the first hours decide the outcome. A virtual-asset incident adds a time-critical step: fund tracing, because exchanges and services can freeze assets only while the trail is fresh. Agree a retainer or an on-call provider before you need one, and know who contacts your regulator. The incident response guide has a 24-hour checklist.

FAQ

Which cybersecurity companies in the UAE help banks stay protected against digital-banking threats?

Banks typically combine a managed service for monitoring with a testing provider for application and API assessments. In our ranking, the cards of Help AG, CPX, Palo Alto Networks and Paladion describe managed detection or platform capabilities relevant to banks, while Paranoid Security's card describes manual testing of web, mobile and API channels. Check each provider's regulator experience.

What does a fintech company need to test before launch?

The customer-facing web and mobile apps, the APIs behind them, the authentication and recovery flows, and any payment integration. Run a retest after fixes, and keep the report for regulators and partners.

Do crypto firms in the UAE need blockchain forensics?

Not as a general rule, but a firm that holds customer assets benefits from the ability to trace funds after theft or fraud and to screen incoming transfers. The FATF Travel Rule, with a threshold of USD/EUR 1,000, shapes compliance work for virtual asset service providers.

Is penetration testing mandatory for banks in the UAE?

Banks supervised by the CBUAE should expect independent testing requirements; the exact wording sits in the current CBUAE rules and your supervisor's guidance. Confirm the clause with your compliance team.

How do I choose between a managed service and a testing specialist?

A managed service watches your systems continuously; a testing specialist finds weaknesses before attackers do. Mature programmes use both. If your budget allows only one, decide whether detection or prevention is your larger gap.
