How an Engagement Usually Runs

Knowing the sequence helps you compare proposals. A typical penetration testing engagement starts with scoping and rules of engagement, followed by access set-up, testing, a draft report, a walkthrough with your team, remediation and a retest. A web application test of moderate size usually takes two to four weeks from scoping to the final report, and network tests take longer depending on infrastructure size. A managed detection service starts with onboarding: connecting log sources, tuning detections and agreeing escalation paths, which can take weeks before the service reaches steady state. Ask each provider for a timeline, and treat a proposal that skips scoping or onboarding as a warning sign.

Common Mistakes Dubai Buyers Make

  • Treating DESC accreditation as a quality guarantee. Accreditation shows that a provider meets a scheme's conditions for a service type; it does not replace reading a sample report.
  • Choosing by headquarters. A provider headquartered elsewhere in the UAE can serve Dubai well, and a Dubai address does not prove delivery depth. Ask where the people who will do the work are based.
  • Ignoring the licence perimeter. DIFC, VARA-regulated and mainland entities face different expectations. A report written for the wrong framework needs rework.
  • Buying a platform when a service is needed. Product vendors in our ranking supply technology; a test or an incident response engagement is a service with its own scope, report and retest.
  • Skipping the data clause. Test data and logs can contain personal data. The contract should say where they are stored and how they are deleted.

How to Choose a Provider in Dubai: Five Checks

  1. Check the DESC register. If accreditation matters to your contract, confirm status and scope on the DESC site, not from a marketing page.
  2. Ask for a sample report. Look for proof-of-concept evidence, CVSS ratings and a remediation plan for each finding.
  3. Confirm your regulatory perimeter. A DIFC firm, a VARA-licensed firm and a mainland company have different expectations. Tell the provider which one you are.
  4. Agree an incident contact. Ask who answers a call at 03:00, in which language and from which location.
  5. Read the data clause. Check where logs and test data are stored, which sub-processors are used and how data is deleted after the project.

Penetration Testing and Incident Response in Dubai

Penetration testing and incident response are the two services where Dubai buyers meet accreditation questions most often. For testing, ask whether the testers who will work on your scope are senior, how much of the work is manual, and whether retesting is included; our penetration testing guide lists the questions. For incident response, ask whether a retainer exists, what the response time is and who contacts the regulator; see the incident response guide.

Tips for Dubai Tenders

If you are writing a tender, state the evidence you will accept rather than a list of brand names. Ask for the accreditation relevant to your scope, a sample report, named senior staff, a retest commitment and the contract clause on data location. Score responses on the same criteria, and ask each bidder to confirm which services are delivered by its own team and which by partners. A tender that asks for evidence is harder to answer with marketing language, and it shortens the shortlist for you.

FAQ

Is DESC accreditation mandatory for Dubai companies?

Not for every company. DESC rules apply to Dubai government entities and organisations in scope of Dubai's information security framework, and Dubai government procurement often asks for accredited providers. A private company outside that scope is not obliged to use an accredited provider, although some clients ask for one.

How do I verify a provider's DESC accreditation?

Check the DESC accredited-provider register and confirm the service type, for example penetration testing or incident response, and the validity period.

What is the difference between DIFC and mainland Dubai for cybersecurity?

DIFC has its own data protection law and its own financial regulator, the DFSA. Mainland entities follow federal rules such as the PDPL and any Dubai rules that apply. Tell the provider where you are licensed so the report maps to the right framework.

Can a provider outside Dubai work with Dubai entities?

Yes, if it meets the client's contract and accreditation requirements. Some providers deliver remotely; some engagements, such as physical testing or on-site incident response, need local presence. Confirm delivery arrangements before you sign.
